Hydrogencarbonate Indicator Experiment

Hydrogencarbonate indicator changes colour based on CO₂ concentration. This experiment shows the effects of photosynthesis and respiration on CO₂ levels.

Colour Key: Hydrogencarbonate Indicator
Yellow = more CO₂ (acidic)
Orange/Red = normal CO₂
Purple = less CO₂ (alkaline)
Tube 1
Plant in bright light
Turns purple
Photosynthesis removes CO₂, making the solution more alkaline.
Tube 2
Plant in dark (wrapped in foil)
Turns yellow
Only respiration occurs (no light for photosynthesis), adding CO₂ and making solution more acidic.
Tube 3
No plant (control)
Stays orange/red
No living organism, so CO₂ levels remain unchanged. This is the control.
Tube 4
Plant + small animal in light
Turns purple
In bright light, the plant's photosynthesis removes more CO₂ than the animal's respiration produces.

This experiment demonstrates that photosynthesis removes CO₂ from the surroundings (turning the indicator purple/alkaline), while respiration adds CO₂ (turning it yellow/acidic). The control confirms that any colour changes are due to the organisms.
