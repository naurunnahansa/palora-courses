Star Lifecycle

Click each stage to learn more. Two paths depend on stellar mass.

Nebula (Stellar Nursery)
A vast cloud of gas (mainly hydrogen) and dust
Nebulae are enormous clouds of gas and dust, sometimes light-years across. Gravity causes regions of higher density to begin collapsing inward. Triggers can include shockwaves from nearby supernovae. Temperature: ~10-50 K. Composition: ~75% hydrogen, ~25% helium.

Protostar
Collapsing gas cloud heats up from gravitational energy
As the gas collapses, gravitational potential energy converts to thermal energy. The core temperature rises to millions of degrees. This stage lasts ~100,000 years. When the core reaches ~15 million K, hydrogen fusion ignites and the star enters the main sequence.

↓
Main Sequence Star
Stable star: fusion radiation pressure balances gravity
The star is in equilibrium: outward radiation pressure from hydrogen fusion balances inward gravitational collapse. Our Sun has been on the main sequence for ~4.6 billion years. Low-mass stars (like the Sun) can last 10+ billion years. High-mass stars burn through fuel in just millions of years. Energy: 4H -> He + energy (E = mc²).

Low Mass (like our Sun)
Red Giant
Outer layers expand, core contracts
Hydrogen fuel in the core runs out. The core contracts and heats up, while outer layers expand and cool (turning red). Helium fusion begins in the core. The star can expand to 100x its original size. Surface temp drops to ~3,000-4,000 K.
↓
Planetary Nebula
Outer layers gently ejected into space
The outer layers are shed, creating a beautiful glowing shell of gas. The hot core is exposed. These nebulae have nothing to do with planets, the name is historical. They disperse over ~10,000 years, enriching space with heavier elements.
↓
White Dwarf
Dense remnant, slowly cools over billions of years
The remaining core is incredibly dense (a teaspoon would weigh tonnes). About the size of Earth but with the mass of the Sun. No fusion occurs; it slowly radiates remaining heat. Eventually becomes a cold black dwarf (none exist yet, the universe is not old enough).
High Mass (> 8 Solar masses)
Red Supergiant
Massive expansion, fuses heavier elements
Massive stars fuse heavier elements in layers: He, C, O, Ne, Si, up to iron. Iron cannot release energy by fusion. The core develops an "onion" structure of fusion shells. Stars like Betelgeuse are red supergiants, up to 1000x the Sun's radius.
↓
Supernova
Catastrophic explosion, outshines entire galaxies
When the iron core can no longer support itself, it collapses in milliseconds. The resulting shockwave blows the outer layers into space at ~10% the speed of light. Creates elements heavier than iron. For a few weeks, a supernova can outshine an entire galaxy. Seed material for new stars and planets.
↓
Neutron Star
If core < 3 solar masses
Protons and electrons are crushed into neutrons. A city-sized object with more mass than the Sun. Density: ~10^17 kg/m³. Can spin hundreds of times per second (pulsars). Has an incredibly strong magnetic field.
Black Hole
If core > 3 solar masses
If the remaining core exceeds ~3 solar masses, nothing can stop the collapse. A singularity forms, surrounded by an event horizon from which not even light can escape. Space and time are severely warped. Detected by their effects on nearby matter and gravitational waves.
